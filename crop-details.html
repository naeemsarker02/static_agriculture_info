Wheat
Varieties

Common varieties include Hard Red Winter, Soft White, and Durum wheat, each suited to different baking and pasta uses.

Cultivation Methods

Wheat is typically drill-seeded in rows, grown as a rain-fed or irrigated crop, and rotated with legumes to maintain soil fertility.

Soil Requirements

Prefers well-drained loamy soil with a pH between 6.0 and 7.0 and moderate organic matter.

Harvesting Techniques

Harvested by combine once the grain moisture drops below 14 percent and the stalks turn golden brown.

Rice
Varieties

Popular varieties include Basmati, Jasmine, and short-grain rice, differing in aroma, texture, and cooking time.

Cultivation Methods

Grown mainly by transplanting nursery-raised seedlings into flooded paddy fields, or by direct seeding in drier systems.

Soil Requirements

Thrives in clay or clay-loam soils that hold water well, with a pH between 5.5 and 6.5.

Harvesting Techniques

Fields are drained about two weeks before harvest, then grain is cut and threshed once around 80 percent of grains turn golden.

Mango
Varieties

Common varieties include Alphonso, Kent, and Tommy Atkins, varying in sweetness, fiber content, and size.

Cultivation Methods

Grown from grafted saplings planted in orchards, pruned annually after harvest to maintain an open canopy.

Soil Requirements

Prefers deep, well-drained sandy loam soil with a pH between 5.5 and 7.5.

Harvesting Techniques

Picked by hand when fruit develops a slight color change and a sweet aroma near the stem, then ripened off the tree.

Banana
Varieties

Common varieties include Cavendish, Plantain, and Lady Finger, differing in size, sweetness, and cooking use.

Cultivation Methods

Propagated from suckers planted in well-spaced rows, requiring consistent watering and wind protection.

Soil Requirements

Grows best in deep, fertile, well-drained soil with a pH between 5.5 and 7.0.

Harvesting Techniques

Bunches are cut by hand when fruit fingers become plump and rounded, then ripened after harvest.

Tomato
Varieties

Common varieties include Roma, Beefsteak, and Cherry tomatoes, varying in size, shape, and use.

Cultivation Methods

Started as seedlings indoors, then transplanted outdoors and staked or caged for support as they grow.

Soil Requirements

Prefers well-drained, fertile soil rich in organic matter with a pH between 6.0 and 6.8.

Harvesting Techniques

Picked by hand when fruit is fully colored but still firm, harvested every few days during peak season.

Potato
Varieties

Common varieties include Russet, Yukon Gold, and Red potatoes, differing in starch content and texture.

Cultivation Methods

Grown from seed potatoes planted in hilled rows, with soil mounded around stems as plants grow.

Soil Requirements

Grows best in loose, well-drained sandy loam soil with a pH between 5.0 and 6.5.

Harvesting Techniques

Dug up once the foliage has died back, usually two to three weeks after the vines yellow and dry.

Cotton
Varieties

Common varieties include Upland cotton and Pima cotton, differing in fiber length and quality.

Cultivation Methods

Direct-seeded in warm soil after the last frost, grown in wide rows with regular irrigation and pest monitoring.

Soil Requirements

Prefers deep, well-drained loamy soil with a pH between 5.8 and 8.0.

Harvesting Techniques

Mechanically picked once bolls have fully opened and fibers have dried in the field.

Sugarcane
Varieties

Common varieties are bred regionally for sugar yield, disease resistance, and climate tolerance.

Cultivation Methods

Propagated from stem cuttings planted in furrows, requiring heavy irrigation and periodic fertilization.

Soil Requirements

Grows best in deep, fertile, well-drained soil with a pH between 6.0 and 7.5.

Harvesting Techniques

Cut close to the ground once stalks mature, typically 10 to 18 months after planting, either by hand or machine.
