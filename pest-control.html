Pest Control Strategies

Integrated Pest Management (IPM) combines natural predators, biological controls, and organic remedies to keep pest populations in check while minimizing harm to crops, beneficial insects, and the environment.

Natural Predators

Ladybugs, lacewings, spiders, and predatory birds feed on aphids, mites, and caterpillars, keeping pest numbers low without chemicals.

Biological Controls

Beneficial bacteria, fungi, and parasitic wasps target specific pests, such as using Bacillus thuringiensis to control caterpillars.

Organic Remedies

Neem oil, insecticidal soap, and diatomaceous earth offer plant-safe treatment options that break down naturally.

Monitoring & Early Detection

Regular monitoring catches infestations early, when they are easiest and cheapest to treat.

Stage What to Do
Monitoring Tips Inspect leaves, stems, and soil weekly, use sticky traps to track flying insects, and keep a simple log of pest sightings by date and location.
Early Detection Signs Look for chewed or discolored leaves, sticky residue from aphids, small holes in fruit, wilting despite adequate water, and visible eggs on leaf undersides.
Effective Treatment Steps Identify the pest correctly, start with the least invasive option such as hand removal or beneficial insects, apply organic sprays if the problem persists, and isolate or remove severely affected plants to stop the spread.
